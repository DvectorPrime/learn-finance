# Four Completed Widget Configurations

## 1. Make the Trade Work

- **ID:** `L01.CT1.G2.I02`
- **Component type:** `drag_drop_trade`
- **Purpose:** Show why barter can fail when a trader does not want what another trader offers.
- **Data:** Four traders form a cycle: rice seller wants shoes; shoemaker wants a phone charger; charger seller wants beans; bean farmer wants rice.
- **Learner action:** Choose the item each trader receives.
- **Validation:** The activity checks whether each person receives the item they want and reports the number of matches.
- **Debrief:** Direct exchange requires the traders' wants to line up; a chain of trades may be difficult to coordinate.

## 2. Build a ₦10,000 Basket

- **ID:** `L01.CT1.G4.I04`
- **Component type:** `allocation_basket`
- **Purpose:** Teach price comparison, arithmetic, scarcity, and trade-offs.
- **Budget:** ₦10,000.
- **Catalogue:** Rice ₦2,500; beans ₦1,800; eggs ₦1,500; cooking oil ₦2,200; tomatoes ₦800; bread ₦1,200; milk ₦1,000; garri ₦900; noodles ₦700.
- **Learner action:** Add/remove quantities and build a basket.
- **Validation:** The total and remaining amount update live; the basket must not exceed ₦10,000.
- **Debrief:** More than one basket may fit the budget. Ask what the learner prioritised and what they had to give up.
- **Data note:** Prices are fictional practice values, not claims about current market prices.

## 3. Budget Shock Simulator

- **ID:** `L17.CT2.G1.I02`
- **Component type:** `scenario_simulator`
- **Purpose:** Practise updating a plan when reality differs from the original budget.
- **Starting income:** ₦75,000.
- **Starting allocations:** Food ₦18,000; transport ₦8,000; housing ₦18,000; data ₦5,000; school/work ₦7,000; savings ₦10,000; family obligations ₦5,000; personal spending ₦4,000. Total: ₦75,000.
- **Shock events:** Transport rises by ₦4,000; a ₦10,000 payment is delayed; or available income falls by ₦15,000.
- **Learner action:** Apply a shock and edit the plan so allocations fit the money actually available.
- **Validation:** Submission is disabled when total allocations exceed available income.
- **Debrief:** Learners explain which priorities they protected and which adjustments were realistic.

## 4. Pick a Realistic Starting Point

- **ID:** `L18.CT1.G1.I03`
- **Component type:** `option_comparison`
- **Purpose:** Encourage a sustainable saving habit without imposing a universal savings rate.
- **Scenario:** Monthly income ₦20,000; essential costs ₦12,000; transport/data ₦5,000; ₦3,000 remains before other necessary costs.
- **Options:**
  - Save ₦500: more flexibility, slower progress.
  - Save ₦1,000: moderate contribution, but check whether ₦2,000 remaining is realistic.
  - Save ₦2,000: faster progress if affordable, but only ₦1,000 remains before other necessary costs.
- **Learner action:** Choose an option and explain the trade-off.
- **Validation:** Requires a selected option and written reasoning; no choice is automatically treated as best for everyone.
- **Debrief:** A good saving amount depends on essential costs, obligations, uncertainty, and the ability to repeat the habit.
